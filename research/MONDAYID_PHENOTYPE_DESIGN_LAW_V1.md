# MONDAYID PHENOTYPE DESIGN LAW V1

Status: CANONICAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE

## Core idea

MondayID is not assembled as a collection of individually attractive UI components.
It is composed as a single phenotype.

Analogy: a person choosing a complete look does not optimize shoes, hair, scent, posture, voice, fabric and lighting independently. Each element changes the meaning of the others. The result is judged as one total field.

Therefore the design unit of MondayID is not the button, card, animation, color, font or screen.
The design unit is the **whole experienced state**.

## Phenotype equation

`P = f(Form, Function, Motion, Sound, Language, Timing, Context, Memory, Host, UserState)`

No variable is allowed to optimize independently if that damages the total phenotype.

## Laws

1. **Whole before component**
   A component may be beautiful and still be rejected if it weakens the total state.

2. **Every detail changes the field**
   Position, typography, motion, latency, haptics, copy, silence, color, density and transition timing are not decoration. Each modifies perception and meaning.

3. **Coherence beats novelty**
   A spectacular isolated interaction is weaker than a coherent system whose every behavior feels inevitable.

4. **Attraction is functional**
   Beauty may pull attention, reduce fear, create curiosity, signal precision, increase trust or make exploration rewarding. If attraction has no cognitive/product function, it is ornamental debt.

5. **Memetic propagation, not malware**
   “Viral” means the product is memorable, recognizable, demonstrable and naturally worth showing to another person. No deceptive, coercive or exploitative growth mechanics are part of the law.

6. **Identity is relational**
   A typeface, surface, icon or sound has no MondayID identity alone. Identity emerges from how all elements behave together across time.

7. **No fixed costume**
   The phenotype can adapt to host, task and user, but its deep invariants remain recognizable. Adaptation must preserve identity rather than become random theming.

## Design consequence

Do not ask:
- What should the home screen look like?
- What color should the button be?
- Should the UI be glass, liquid, flat, dark, spatial or minimal?

Ask:
- What should the user feel, understand and be able to do in this exact state?
- What total sensory/cognitive composition produces that result with the least contradiction?
- Which elements are necessary for the whole, and which are merely attractive fragments?

## Product test

A MondayID scene passes only if:
- removing one important element makes the total experience measurably worse;
- changing one element can be explained in terms of the whole system;
- the scene remains coherent across motion, interaction, language and state transition;
- the user remembers the experience as one thing, not as a list of features;
- the result is desirable enough to be shown voluntarily to someone else.

## Canonical phrase

**Design the organism, not the outfit pieces.**

The interface is the visible phenotype of the cognitive system underneath it.
